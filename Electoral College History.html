  • How Does One Become President by Losing the Popular Vote But Wins Anyway?

In the last twenty-five years there have been several cases where a presidential candidate won the presidency but lost the popular vote. In the year 2000 it was George W. Bush and Al Gore both running for President and the whole election came down to one battleground state: Florida! At first the television networks declared Gore the winner then retracted and stated that Bush was the winner. Due to the fact that the margin of the vote was less than one-half of a percent of the number of votes in Florida, a recount was legitimately allowed to occur. After the recount, Bush was still declared the winner however the margin of the vote was less than before. Gore applied for a manual recount in selected counties that were qualified under this provision. The deadline for recounting kept getting pushed out further and further into late November. After many speeches, appeals and filed objections, and contested ballots that the Florida Supreme Court failed to identify, it all came down to the US Supreme Court issuing the Florida Supreme Court a deadline of December 12, 2000, for creating standards for this recount. During this process, the manual recounts were stopped. On the day of this deadline the US Supreme Court validated the original report, and Bush was declared the winner of Florida’s twenty-five electoral votes and the 2000 presidential election.

In 1984 the incumbent Ronald Reagan received 525 electoral votes and won by a landslide while his opponent Walter Mondale only received 13 electoral votes. An interesting fact is that Walter Mondale’s running mate, Geraldine Ferraro was the first woman Vice Presidential candidate ever.

In 2020 the incumbent Donald J. Trump lost the election to Joseph Biden. The electoral vote certification, usually done in January, was disrupted in Washington DC due to violent unrest in and around the Capitol building on January 6, 2021.
